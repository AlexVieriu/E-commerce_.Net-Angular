The security benefit: a stolen session token expires predictably regardless of usage. The UX cost: surprise logouts. Decision: accepted the cost because e-commerce with stored addresses and order history is high-value — PCI-DSS-aligned absolute expiry is defensible to users ("for your security, sessions expire after 24 hours").</p><p><b>2. <code class="c">SameSite = Strict</code> (security win, UX cost):</b></p><p>Breaks "click email link → land logged in" flows. A user clicking their order confirmation email lands on the login page even though they have an active session. Decision: accepted because this project has no OAuth and the target audience (B2C shoppers) rarely use email links to navigate back to active sessions. The full CSRF protection outweighs the occasional login prompt.</p><p><b>Framework for the conversation:</b></p><p>Quantify the UX cost (how many users hit this? how often?) and the security benefit (what attack does this prevent? how likely?). A CSRF attack is low-probability but high-impact. A surprise logout is high-probability but low-impact. The math favors security here.</p></div></div><div class="rf"><p class="rf-label">🚩 Red Flag — shallow answer</p><div class="ac"><p>"Security always wins." That's not a tradeoff — it's an avoidance of the decision. If security always won, every app would require hardware tokens, biometric re-auth every 5 minutes, and no persistent sessions. Real security decisions are cost-benefit analysis, not absolutes.</p></div></div><div class="gf"><p class="gf-label">✓ Green Flag — pro insight</p><div class="ac"><p>Mentioning that the right answer changes with business context — a banking app and a recipe site have different risk profiles. For banking: <code class="c">SlidingExpiration = false</code>, short absolute expiry (15 min), <code class="c">SameSite = Strict</code>, re-auth for sensitive actions. For a recipe site: <code class="c">SlidingExpiration = true</code>, long expiry, <code class="c">SameSite = Lax</code>. Neither is wrong — the tradeoff is explicit and context-driven.</p></div></div>
